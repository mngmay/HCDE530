# Mini Project 1 — Competency Claims

**C5 — Data analysis with pandas.** I answered three research questions about Ravelry pattern popularity using pandas on a 7,332-row dataset I collected from the Ravelry API. Because each pattern appeared once per month, I used `drop_duplicates(subset="pattern_id")` to get an honest 609-pattern sample before counting anything, then used `value_counts`, `groupby().agg()`, `nlargest`, and `pd.crosstab(normalize="index")` to rank categories, compare seasonal yarn-weight and fiber mixes as percentages, and identify the most popular designers. I also derived a new column (publication season) with `pd.to_datetime` and a month-to-season mapping when the original season column proved unusable.

**C6 — Data visualization.** I built my charts with Plotly Express, choosing chart types to fit each comparison: grouped percentage bars for seasonal weight/fiber mixes (percentages rather than raw counts, since seasons had different sample sizes) and a final horizontal stacked bar where each designer's bar is built from their individual patterns. That chart's title states the finding itself — "One viral pattern can rival a whole catalog" — and the segment structure lets a reader see total popularity and catalog depth in a single view, with labeled axes and hoverable pattern names.

**C7 — Critical evaluation and professional judgment.** My analysis surfaced a serious hidden flaw: every season contained exactly 1,833 rows, and by checking the pattern IDs I confirmed the API's month filter had silently failed, making all four seasons identical. Rather than report fake seasonal trends, I documented the issue in my Data Profile, pivoted to publication date as a defensible season proxy, and dropped the designer-experience metric entirely once I realized it was computed only from patterns in my sample. Throughout the notebook I stated concessions and limitations explicitly — including verifying AI-suggested numbers and fixes against the actual data before accepting them — and framed my conclusions as directional trends rather than market shares.
